# explica-clar: evidence

Everything here comes from search-result summaries and abstracts, not from full papers. Nothing was measured live for this skill. This file keeps the loaded instructions in `SKILL.md` compact.

| Rule | Study and size | Finding | Strength and caveats |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 SUMMARY FIRST | Cochrane plain-language-summary trial, 143 lay readers ([statschat](https://www.statschat.org.nz/2014/08/02/when-in-doubt-randomise-4/)); a second trial, 813 midwifery students | 53% vs 18% understood benefits, harms and quality of evidence; the second trial found no significant difference | MIXED. The CDC Clear Communication Index also puts the main message first: design rationale, no outcome data found |
| 2 FEW CHUNKS | Rey et al., meta-analysis of the segmenting effect, 56 investigations ([doi](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S10648-018-9456-4)); AHRQ Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit | Small-to-medium gains in retention and transfer; the toolkit advises limiting the number of teaching points | GOOD. The best number of segments is unsettled |
| 3 ONE REAL EXAMPLE | Barbieri et al. 2023, meta-analysis of worked examples, 55 studies | g≈0.48 on mathematics performance; the worked-example effect is described mainly for novices | GOOD, but from mathematics |
| 4 ANALOGY WITH A MAP | Structure-mapping theory; Alfieri et al. 2013, meta-analysis of case comparisons, 57 experiments, 336 tests; one study of 40 patients | Highlighting similarities, then stating the principle, helped; a visual analogy was understood by 83% vs 63% for text alone | MEDIUM, partly inference. No pooled meta-analysis of explanatory analogies was found |
| 5 ONE FLAT DIAGRAM | Meta-analysis of pictorial health information; Noetel et al., overview of 29 reviews, 1,189 studies, 78,177 participants; BPMN experiment, 60 practitioners and 140 students; the 40-patient study | Knowledge and recall improved, no effect on adherence; spatial/temporal contiguity and signalling among the largest effects; flattened models understood best; a line graph (60%) did not beat text (63%) | GOOD for pictures and contiguity, MEDIUM for flat diagrams (one domain) |
| 6 SIGNAL WHAT MATTERS | Noetel et al. overview (signalling) | Signalling among the largest effects | GOOD |
| 7 MATCH THE READER | Expertise-reversal effect (Kalyuga et al. 2003; Rey and Buchwald); the summaries used give no study sizes | Explanations that help novices slow experts down | GOOD in small lab experiments. CDC «Everyday Words» is design rationale only |
| 8 CHECK UNDERSTANDING | Talevski et al. 2020, systematic review ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32287296/)) | Teach-back effective in 19 of 20 studies | MEDIUM. Study quality uneven |

Stories and narratives are not used as a clarity technique: the studies found measure persuasion, not understanding.
