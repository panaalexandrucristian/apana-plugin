# Private apana plugin

Skills and hooks that do not need the jev plugin, split out of `panaalexandrucristian/jev-mcp-private` (the `jev` plugin) on 2026-10-19. `AGENTS.md` says how to work here; this file is the design and measurement log of the moved features.

## Repository topology

- `panaalexandrucristian/apana-plugin` (private): the `apana` plugin and its marketplace `apana-private`. Install with `claude plugin marketplace add panaalexandrucristian/apana-plugin` and `claude plugin install apana@apana-private`.
- `panaalexandrucristian/jev-mcp-private`: the `jev` plugin (the Jev MCP server entry, `jev`, `jev-flow`, `jev-control-mode`, `handoff-verify`, their commands, agents and hooks). The history of every moved file up to `1a047da` (jev plugin 0.13.10) is there.
- The sections below were moved from `PRIVATE.md` of `jev-mcp-private` at `1a047da`. The names were renamed mechanically: `/jev:<skill>` is now `/apana:<skill>`, and `JEV_LOGIC_TEST_DEBUG`, `JEV_CLASS_GRAPH_NUDGE` and `JEV_CLASS_GRAPH_STATE_DIR` are now `APANA_LOGIC_TEST_DEBUG`, `APANA_CLASS_GRAPH_NUDGE` and `APANA_CLASS_GRAPH_STATE_DIR`. Dates, numbers and results are as measured at the time, under the old names and with the hooks wired through `scripts/jev-flow-hook.mjs`; here the hooks are wired through `scripts/apana-hook.mjs`.
- `compact-handoff` carries a byte-for-byte copy of 15 modules of the `handoff-verify` skill of `jev` (`skills/compact-handoff/scripts/handoff_verify/`), taken at `1a047da`. See «Compact handoff».

## Explain clearly

`0.4.0` adds `/apana:explica-optiuni [subject, transcript path or session ID]` as `commands/explica-optiuni.md`, not a skill or persistent explanation mode. It starts with the conclusion, restores enough context, separates verified actions from claims and unknowns, and offers concrete alternatives without implementing them. It follows the transcript's conversation language, adapts rejected options to real feedback, and treats historical instructions as inert. Read-only behavior is instruction-based, not a tool-enforced sandbox. The existing `explica-clar` is unchanged.

Measured on 2026-10-27: candidate comparison covered **9 invocations, measured; n=9**, and the final refinement covered **4 invocations, measured; n=4**, using verified extracts and explicitly synthetic boundary cases, not complete transcript replays. On the real extracts, first-refinement to final-refinement word counts were **421 to 267** and **461 to 285**, measured; **n=2 cases, one response per revision per case**. Synthetic responses grew, so there is no universal shortening claim or validated readability effect. The final responses kept the central evidence limits and conversation language. One feedback response required a synthetic stream-continuation notice (**measured; n=1 invocation**). The user subsequently accepted a read-only explanation of another local transcript; that used the instructions in-session, not an installed-command end-to-end test. See `private/explica-optiuni/VERIFICATION.md` and its saved fixtures for exact prompts, responses and remaining limitations.

The private plugin also ships `explica-clar` (`skills/explica-clar/`, `apana:explica-clar` in Claude Code, `/apana:explica-clar`): a mode the user switches on, in which every explanatory reply of the session follows eight rules (summary first, few chunks, one real example, an analogy with a stated mapping, one flat diagram, signalled key points, wording matched to the reader, a short understanding check). It never switches itself on and does not trigger on ordinary questions.

Switch it on with `/apana:explica-clar` or a request such as «explică clar», «explain clearly» or «explică pe înțelesul meu». Switch it off with e.g. «oprește explica-clar» / «stop explaining clearly»; ask whether it is on with e.g. «explica-clar este activ?» / «is explica-clar on?». A new session starts with it off. An explicit instruction of the user (shorter, more technical, no diagram) outranks it. Since 0.13.4 it replies in English, also when the user writes another language, in a style based on ASD-STE100: short sentences with one idea each, words from a community copy of the Issue 9 vocabulary (not the official PDF), and the technical part of a reply is called «Technical data». The style is always on while the mode is on, so there is no separate switch for it.

It is instructions only: no hook, command, script or state file, so the persistence through the session is an instruction to the model, not a mechanism. It is Claude Code only: `opencode-plugin.js` does not register it. The sources and figures of each rule are in [`skills/explica-clar/reference/evidence.md`](skills/explica-clar/reference/evidence.md), not in `SKILL.md`, so the instructions loaded into the session stay compact (as `jev-control-mode` and `jev-flow` keep their details in `reference/`). The research comes from education and health-communication studies, read as abstracts and search summaries, not full papers and not software documentation. Measured on 2026-10-14 with Sonnet and five questions (git rebase, undo a commit, Android rotation, a REST API, port 8080), one reply per case, checkers that read word forms and not meaning (`ste_lint.py`, `ste_vocab.py`, kept outside the repository): replies of subagents that read the skill file had 8 structure violations (sentence or paragraph too long, contractions) with the old text and 0 with the new one, and a mean of 22.4 and 8.6 distinct words outside the vocabulary. In 34 real `claude -p` sessions (the skill installed as a project skill, no tools) two identical runs of the new text gave a mean of 7.8 and 9.8, so a difference below about 2 is noise; renaming «Technical details» to «Technical data» removed the word «details» from 5 of 5 replies (it was in 5 of 5) and did not lower the total. After «no STE» in a second turn the reply stopped following the style, with or without a line that names the command (one session each). Whether it triggers only on request, persists through a long session or improves how well a reader understands is unverified. The offline test is `node --test private/explica-clar/test/`.

The private plugin also ships `logic-test-debug` (`skills/logic-test-debug/`, `apana:logic-test-debug` in Claude Code): rules for reading Boolean conditions, testing combinations of conditions and debugging in a fixed sequence, each rule marked CLEAR (a measured effect in its source), DESCRIPTIVE (observed or proposed, no outcome test) or INFERENCE (not in any source). The rules come from three sources: Baron et al. (EASE 2024, 205 professionals, reading time of Boolean expressions with negations), the NIST combinatorial-testing material by Kuhn (failures triggered by few interacting conditions; the 2004 paper itself was not read) and Maleti and Nel (SACLA 2025, a seven-step debugging model from six lecturers; a pre-review manuscript with no outcome test). The sources, pages, numbers, exceptions and guardrails of every rule are in [`skills/logic-test-debug/reference/evidence.md`](skills/logic-test-debug/reference/evidence.md), not in `SKILL.md` (at most 90 lines).

**Activation.** Automatic, on code prompts only. In Claude Code, `scripts/apana-hook.mjs` loads `private/logic-test-debug/hook.mjs` by a guarded dynamic import on `UserPromptSubmit` (the hook command, its 10 s timeout and `hooks/hooks.json` are unchanged); when a local check, with no model call, finds the prompt to be about writing, changing, reading, testing or debugging code, it adds one directive of about 430 characters to `additionalContext`, joined to any context the other subsystems produced. Other prompts, other events and a failing or missing new module add nothing and leave the existing output unchanged. In OpenCode, `opencode-plugin.js` sets `private/logic-test-debug/opencode.mjs` up in its own try/catch after the flow: it registers the skill beside `jev` and queues the same directive in a `prompt` hook that a `context` hook delivers in every model request of the turn, until the next prompt of the session clears or renews it. Each OpenCode capability is feature-detected and degrades with one fixed diagnostic and no prompt text, and the steps depend on each other: a failed skill registration (or an existing skill with the same id) disables the directive, because it would name a skill that is not there, and without the `context` hook the `prompt` producer is not registered; the existing components are never touched, and the flow's read-only locator subagent gets no directive.

**Why a directive.** In the Android A/B runs a skill chosen only by its description, with one hint per request, was never loaded in 2 of 2 headless runs (`docs/jev-flow-findings.md`, F1). After the directive fix the skill loaded unprompted in 1 of 1 run, but the change combined several parts, so the directive's own contribution is not isolated, and the new check is not validated by it.

**The local check** (`private/logic-test-debug/check.mjs`) is lexical, in English and Romanian: an affirmative coding or debugging intent (an action word with programming context in the same clause, or a standalone «debug»/«depanează»), or a code artifact (a fenced block in a code language or with recognisable syntax, a stack trace or compiler diagnostic, a source file name or inline code together with a request word; a source file name in the same clause also counts as programming context for a request verb such as «repair» or «implement»). Negated intent (also «I don't want you to write code»), quoted words, an explicit limit to prose and a translation or quotation add nothing, and neither does a request about a CV, a recipe, a poem or a video whose only code-like words are ambiguous ones (program, script, method, function, python, cod). Known false positives remain, for example «Develop a training program for new sales hires». Stack traces and compiler output are matched line by line with bounded patterns (an earlier version was quadratic: a 64 KiB prompt of one long path-like word took 2 to 5 s; it now takes about 1 ms, measured). It rejects non-string input and anything over 64 KiB (65,536 UTF-8 bytes) and returns no directive when its 25 ms cooperative budget has expired; synchronous code cannot be preempted, so no hard wall-clock guarantee is claimed. It cannot understand natural language: false matches and misses remain possible (for example «Debug my sleep schedule» matches).

**Switch.** `APANA_LOGIC_TEST_DEBUG`, read from the same environment as `JEV_FLOW`: absent means on; `on`, `1` or `true` (trimmed, case-insensitive) mean on; `off`, `0`, `false`, an empty value and every unrecognised value mean off. Unlike the opt-in `JEV_FLOW`, the default is on, because automatic activation was the requirement. **A `/goal` that is still active goes to the new session (0.13.7).** A handoff made while a `/goal` was running did not bring the goal to the new session. What the research found (observations on 8 real transcripts and the Claude Code documentation): the transcript records the life of a goal as `attachment` records of type `goal_status`: `met` false with `sentinel` true is the setting, `met` false with a `reason` is a check that found the condition unmet, `met` true is achieved, and `met` true with `sentinel` true is `/goal clear`; the system record «Goal paused …» marks a paused goal. An active goal comes back on `--resume` of the same session and never in a new one. The documentation has no `--goal` option and no way to set a goal from a hook, and the model cannot set one; the only way is the command, and a prompt that starts with `/goal` (`claude '/goal …'`) runs as that command. `make_context.py` (`goal_of`) reads the transcript: a goal is active when its last record is a setting or an unmet check; an achieved or cleared goal, a `failed` mark, a `/clear` after it and a newer `/goal` (which replaces it) end or replace it; sidechain records and lines that do not parse are skipped. For an active goal, `HANDOFF.md` gets the section «Goal activ (/goal)» after the answer to give: the condition quoted (through the sanitizer, a handoff path in it masked), the state (when it was set, the number of unmet checks, paused or not), the last unmet reason quoted as text of the system, and what the new session does with it (it does not set a second goal, the goal is the user's request only within its condition, the answer has a fifth point, what is still missing from the condition, and after it the session goes on with the goal without waiting). The script prints an `active goal:` line and, as the LAST line, a start command `claude '/goal <condition on one line> (Continuare dintr-un handoff: …)'` whose prompt keeps the condition first and unchanged and then names the `HANDOFF.md`; the line before it is the plain command. If the whole is longer than 4000 characters (the limit the script uses for a goal condition) only the plain command is printed, with a note, and the new session prints the `/goal` line for the user to type. `--no-goal` turns it off, `--cut` carries the goal too, and an OpenCode session has none. `SKILL.md` tells the model to give the last line as the start command and to say that the goal of the old session stays active until the user types `/goal clear` there, so two sessions do not work on it at once. Tests: 27 new cases in the self test of `make_context.py` (the life of a goal: set, unmet checks, pause, achieved, cleared, replaced, `/clear`, sidechain, noise; the section; the command), one end-to-end test (a condition with quotes, `$`, a backtick and `{}` passed through a shell) that fails on the script of 0.13.6 (checked), and one test of `SKILL.md`; no systematic combination coverage of the conditions of a goal (t not set).

## Same-session compaction

`0.4.0` adds `/apana:compact-session`: a skill-scoped Stop request and native hook compact the active snapshot without clearing or restarting the session. It reuses the existing textual preparation and round-trip verification, archives the original snapshot and recoverable blobs, and installs only a verified smaller replacement. A verified nonshrinking result is a no-op, not invalid output. Success, no-op and failure are delivered to the next accepted prompt; a rejected prompt does not consume the report. The native API is unavailable in headless runs on the tested build; no fallback to the standard summarizer or live transcript editing is attempted. Verification preserves prepared text, not every snapshot byte: sanitization remains active and thinking is archive-only. No `compact-handoff` source or vendored verifier was changed.

Measured on 2026-10-27: **n=2 saved real snapshots** replayed against old and corrected reporting installed identical text for the successful case and no replacement for the nonshrinking case. The change makes the no-op report specific and delivers outcomes to the model; it does not change the compression algorithm. The reporting regression was RED for **10 added cases, measured; n=10**, with **4 existing passes, measured; n=4**; the integrated native suite passed **14 tests, measured; n=14**. These tests and replay evidence do not establish token savings or availability on every engine build. See `private/compact-session/VERIFICATION.md` for the real-session evidence and limitations.

`0.5.0` also fixes a failure of `0.4.0` on current builds: native `tool_use` blocks carry `caller: {type: "direct"}`, which the snapshot validator did not list, so every compaction in a session with a tool call failed closed with `unsupported fields at messages[3].content[1]: caller`. The field is now accepted when absent or exactly `{type: "direct"}`; any other value, including `null` and a call made by another tool, still fails closed because it would otherwise be dropped without a trace. Measured on 2026-10-27: **n=1 real session** (74 messages, 33 tool calls, all with `caller`, measured) was rebuilt from its saved records; `0.4.0` failed with the error above, the fixed script compacted it from 94,053 to 36,548 characters with a round trip reported IDENTICAL. The new test covers all 22 caller x `cache_control` cases (11 caller values x 2), measured; n=22, and is RED without the fix and GREEN with it. The snapshot was rebuilt from session records, not captured by the native hook, so the live replacement is not shown by this evidence.

## Diff review

**`review-diff` (`0.7.0`).** An explicitly invoked skill reviews each changed hunk and new class against its purpose and the project's existing conventions, explains the judgment, fixes justified issues and reviews the resulting diff again. **`review-diff` (`0.8.0`).** Changes to the skill text, which are instructions; the facts about the diff come from a script (`diff-target.mjs`, below).
- With no argument it reviews nothing and asks "What should I review?" (uncommitted changes, a commit, a PR, something else), then waits for the next prompt. ## claude-auto

**Hook-free managed mode (`0.7.0`).** `claude-auto --managed` explicitly starts a separate stream-json text interface with local cooperation; the default native-terminal path is unchanged. The launcher owns admission of human messages and stops assistance before forwarding them. The local MCP bridge exposes bounded cooperation actions, preserving generation, task revision and lease checks, and rejects stale results after withdrawal or supersession. Handoffs occur between completed turns, after the owner process closes. This mode adds no launcher hooks or permission grants, denies actions requiring interactive approval, and requires organizational permission for the launcher and local MCP. Existing configured hooks and settings remain subject to policy. Native slash commands, anchor/resume selectors and `CLAUDE_AUTO_NEXT_ARGS` are unsupported. Installation includes the runtime, bridge and offline tests; uninstall removes the owned files and retains the registry. See `skills/install-claude-auto/references/managed-mode.md` for supported use and limits. Live CLI compatibility and organizational-policy enforcement have not been verified; this release does not install or activate the mode. Integration review found that treating the entire note as history contradicted its skill-reload instructions. The replacement prompt now explicitly follows that section, keeps historical actions non-authorizing, and addresses the new human request or automated notification instead of the note's summary-and-wait instruction.

**Session cooperation (`0.6.0`).** `CLAUDE_AUTO_COOPERATE=1` opts interactive wrapper sessions, including supervised subscription sessions, into the bundled local cooperation plugin; bootstrap and pass-through launches do not participate. `CLAUDE_AUTO_COOP_DB` selects an absolute registry path, defaulting to `~/.claude/cooperation/registry.sqlite3`. Sessions publish bounded delegations, and an explicitly idle session contributes through a separate, tool-less model call containing only the delegated snapshot. Results are attributed and require owner acceptance or rejection; the owner verifies and applies proposals under existing permissions. New owner prompts cancel assistance and supersede the task revision; generation, revision and lease checks reject stale contributions. Wrapper-driven handoffs retain logical membership and owner work while changing physical sessions. Manual clear or in-process resume disables cooperation; unmatched or ambiguous turn association fails closed. A final answer alone does not mark an owner idle: explicit finish is required, with outstanding delegations resolved. Sharing is among opted-in sessions using the same local database, not restricted to a repository. Peer agreement grants no user authorization. Updating the plugin alone does not update installed launchers; rerun the installer. Global settings are unchanged, and uninstall retains the registry.

Automatic handoff prompts now explicitly continue the original task rather than request a handoff summary, preserving the user's final-output requirements. This wrapper behavior applies independently of cooperation opt-in.

Measured on 2026-10-28 in the contributing worktree: the final integrated scenario passed the unchanged functional, exact-answer and isolation checks (n=1 successful run), including context rotation, composer preemption, priority-owner work, automatic re-claim, attributed acceptance and explicit finish. Earlier exact-format failures remain failures (measured n=2 prior runs). Correlated upstream closure after preemption is transport evidence, not proof that provider-internal computation stopped; payload inspection covers the dedicated OpenAI proxy, not direct-provider payloads. The formatting regression failed before repair and passed afterward (measured n=1 test with n=4 pending-count subcases; t=1 is INFERENCE for the single input dimension). ## Variant check

`apana:variant-check` (since `0.1.2`) is a rule and a script. The rule: before writing or changing a matcher or filter over text records, list every distinct variant of the matched token that occurs in the real data and handle each one; afterwards diff the output against the old output on real data and explain every difference. `skills/variant-check/scripts/variants.mjs` does the first step mechanically (distinct variants with counts and the first location; skips `.git`, `node_modules` and files with a NUL byte), with 12 tests in `scripts/test/variants.test.mjs`. `reference/evidence.md` next to the skill holds the measurement.

**Measured on 2026-10-19.** One task, `t2`: a change to the marker matcher of `compact-handoff`, whose tests (30) do not cover the label `[tool_result ERROR]`; only the comparison of the program's output on the real transcript with the reference commit catches a solution that forgets it. `claude -p`, sonnet-5-5, effort medium, a fresh session from a handoff. Output equal to the reference, by how the rule reached the model: no rule 1 of 2 (n = 2); appended to the system prompt, generic wording, 2 of 2 (n = 2); `UserPromptSubmit` hook with `additionalContext` 1 of 3 (n = 3); skill available 2 of 3 (n = 3; whether the skill was invoked is unknown, the runs keep no tool-call log). Two more runs with the example in the prompt are left out (contaminated). Cost per run 0.33 to 0.43 USD (MEASURED, `total_cost_usd`). The samples are too small to rank the channels: they show that the rule can work through the system prompt and that the hook was not better than no rule. Documentation (as summarised by a sub-agent, not re-read): no settings key or variable appends to the system prompt, `--append-system-prompt` is a flag only, which is why `claude-auto` carries it; `additionalContext` should be factual rather than imperative (whether that explains the hook result: not tested). Not known: other tasks, models and efforts; whether an output style of a plugin (`force-for-plugin`) works as well as the flag.

## Guarded implementation tasks

The current implementation is `/apana:scope <task>` in an ordinary Claude session, not a standalone planner/launcher. It starts read-only investigation; the model proposes exact production/test paths and test commands, the user approves the nonce, and hooks gate subsequent writes and commands. New tasks revoke prior approval. Repository-answerable questions require continued investigation; ambiguous user intent requires a question. Mapping paths does not prove test semantics or prevent unrelated edits inside an approved file: the model and user review must check behavior. See README and `skills/scope/SKILL.md` for the live contract.

The old `install-claude-scope` skill and launcher sources have been removed. The legacy uninstaller remains for old installations and does not remove runs/reports. No local installation is removed by updating these sources.

**Limits.** There is no independent clone, OS sandbox, automatic test provenance or semantic patch audit in the new hook workflow. Approved runners execute project code with user privileges. Symlink checks reject preexisting unsafe components but cannot exclude concurrent path replacement by local processes between the check and tool execution. Hook timeouts, disabled hooks and processes outside Claude are outside the guarantee. Handler exceptions block submitted prompts/tools, rather than falling back to authorization.

## Jev decision loop

`apana:jev-decision-loop` (since `0.1.12`, `skills/jev-decision-loop/`) is a mode the user switches on (`/apana:jev-decision-loop`, «folosește Jev pentru toate deciziile», «let Jev decide»), in which Jev takes every decision of the session: before each tool call the model writes 2-5 concrete next steps and the facts so far, `scripts/jev.mjs` asks Jev to pick one, and the model does exactly that one. It is the only part of this repository that calls Jev, and it does so without the jev plugin: the owner chose (2026-10-25) a portable skill, switched on only on request, over the direct API only, shipped in this plugin. The aim is a cheap model (haiku or sonnet at effort low) that lists the steps while Jev picks them.

**Where things live.** `SKILL.md` (the loop, the request format, the verdict rules); `scripts/jev.mjs` (one request as `key: text` lines on stdin, because Claude Code refuses a Bash command with a brace next to a quote, so a JSON request never ran; options become one Jev choice question, each `gate` one probability question; `GO` needs at least 0.6 and a lead of 0.2, a gate is true at 0.7 or above and false at 0.3 or below; it refuses requests that look like a secret or are over 24,000 characters; exit codes 0 answered, 2 bad request, 3 refused, 4 unavailable; every answered decision goes to `~/.claude/jev-decision-loop/decisions.jsonl` without its state); `scripts/register-jev-key.sh` (stores `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` or `OPENROUTER_API_KEY` in the macOS Keychain as `claude-code-jev-typesafe` / `claude-code-jev-openrouter`). The default model is `typesafe/jev-1.13`: `jev-1.14` and `~typesafe/jev-latest` were blocked by the OpenRouter guardrail of this account on 2026-10-25. Tests: `node --test skills/jev-decision-loop/scripts/test/*.test.mjs`, 37 tests (counted 2026-10-26), offline (a local fake server stands for the provider).

**The gate** (`scripts/gate-hook.mjs`, the `hooks` of the skill's frontmatter, which Claude Code registers when the skill is loaded and keeps for the rest of the session): `PreToolUse` on every tool refuses a call that no Jev decision allowed (the `jev.mjs` calls themselves pass); `PostToolUse` on Bash reads the verdict of a `jev.mjs` call, and `GO`, or an `UNSURE` leaning to an option of a decision without gates, allows exactly one tool call; `Stop` refuses, once, the end of a turn unless the last decision picked the reply (`Answer the user` with the `done` gate, or `Ask the user`). `jev.mjs --off` (the user said stop) switches it off until the next `--check`; subagents are left alone; any error lets the call through. The one allowed call is a file per session, taken with `rename`: two `unlink`s of one file from two processes both succeeded in 10 of 30 tries in the Bash sandbox and 6 of 30 outside it, `rename` in 0 of 30 (measured 2026-10-25 on this Mac, APFS, n = 30 per row); with `unlink` the test of two parallel calls failed 4 of 5 runs, with `rename` 0 of 20 (measured).
